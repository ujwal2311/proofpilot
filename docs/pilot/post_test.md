# ProofPilot pilot — post-test (Form B)

Participant ID: ______________

Work alone. No notes, no tool, no discussion. Five questions, five minutes. This form is
equivalent in structure and difficulty to the pre-test, with different letters and topics, so it
does not simply test whether you remember the earlier answers.

**Q1.** You have two statements on a board:

    c1:  D ∨ ¬C
    c2:  C

What single new statement follows from resolving `c1` and `c2` together?

&nbsp;

**Q2.** You have:

    c1:  ¬D
    c2:  D

What do `c1` and `c2` resolve to?

&nbsp;

**Q3.** Someone tries to resolve these two statements:

    c1:  P ∨ Q
    c2:  R ∨ S

Can they be resolved? If not, why not, in one sentence.

&nbsp;

**Q4.** Premises: "If the server is down, the alert fires" and "The server is down."
Conclusion: "The alert fires."

Does the conclusion follow from the premises? (yes / no)

&nbsp;

**Q5.** Premises: "If the shuttle is late, the meeting slips" and "The meeting slipped."
Conclusion: "The shuttle was late."

Does the conclusion follow from the premises? (yes / no)

---

## Answer key (facilitator only — do not show the participant)

1. `D` (resolve on `C`: cancel `C`/`¬C`, keep `D`)
2. `[]` — the empty clause (direct contradiction)
3. No — they share no complementary literal (nothing in `c1` is the negation of anything in `c2`)
4. Yes (modus ponens)
5. No (affirming the consequent — the meeting could have slipped for another reason)

Score: 1 point per correct answer, out of 5.
